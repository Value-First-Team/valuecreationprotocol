## What is the Three-Org Model?

The Three-Org Model is an organizational architecture designed for Value-Led Growth. It replaces traditional functional silos with three unified organizations, each with clear purpose and accountability.

**The Three Orgs:**

1. **Customer Org** — Everyone who creates and delivers value to clients
2. **Operations Org** — AI-powered coordination that enables the Customer Org
3. **Finance Org** — Resource stewardship and value accounting

**The Core Principle:**

Traditional organizations fragment value creation across functional silos—marketing generates, sales closes, delivery implements, account management retains. Each optimizes their stage while customers experience handoffs, context loss, and organizational friction.

The Three-Org Model eliminates that complexity. The Customer Org owns the entire Value Path. The Operations Org handles coordination complexity. Finance stewards resources. Three orgs. Three leaders. One mission: maximize value created and received across all relationships.

## The Three Organizations

### Customer Org

**Purpose:** Everyone who touches the value journey—from first signal recognition through championship. No artificial splits between "getting" customers and "keeping" customers.

**What the Customer Org Owns:**
- Client relationship development and stewardship
- Live session delivery—discovery, coaching, training, facilitation
- Technical implementation and configuration
- Methodology application and framework delivery
- Thought leadership and market education
- The entire Value Path from Audience through Champion

**Key Principle:** No handoffs between Value Path stages. Relationships are stewarded continuously, not transferred between functional specialists.

**Who Belongs Here:**
- Everyone who creates value directly with and for clients
- Value Stewards (the full-journey relationship owners)
- Technical delivery specialists
- Methodology experts
- Anyone whose work directly touches customer value creation

**What Doesn't Belong Here:**
- Internal operations and coordination
- System management and platform maintenance
- Financial administration
- Functions that don't directly touch customer value

**Leadership:** Chief Customer Officer (or Chief Value Officer)—stewards the portfolio of relationships across all Value Path stages.

---

### Operations Org

**Purpose:** AI-powered coordination and enablement that makes the Customer Org effective at creating value.

**What the Operations Org Owns:**
- AI systems and tool infrastructure
- Data quality and integration
- Process efficiency and automation
- Platform reliability and performance
- Project coordination and status tracking
- Documentation synthesis and knowledge management
- System and platform management
- Quality assurance and process optimization

**Key Principle:** Operations should be invisible to clients. They experience seamless coordination; they don't see the machinery behind it.

**Who Belongs Here:**
- AI Operations Lead (Claude or equivalent)
- Platform and system administrators
- Integration specialists
- Process designers
- Anyone whose work enables the Customer Org

**What Doesn't Belong Here:**
- Client relationship ownership
- Revenue generation activities
- Financial decision-making
- Direct customer interaction

**Leadership:** Chief Operations Officer—builds and maintains the infrastructure that enables value creation.

**The AI-Native Distinction:**

In traditional organizations, operations requires large teams managing coordination complexity manually. In the Three-Org Model, AI handles coordination at scale while humans focus on judgment, strategy, and edge cases. This is why the Operations Org can be small (or even a single AI with human oversight) while enabling sophisticated capability.

---

### Finance Org

**Purpose:** Resource stewardship and value accounting—not just tracking money, but measuring value delivered.

**What the Finance Org Owns:**
- Revenue distribution based on contribution
- Cash flow management and financial planning
- Investment prioritization and resource allocation
- Compliance and risk management
- Value accounting alongside traditional financial metrics

**Key Principle:** Measure value delivered, not just revenue captured. This changes investment decisions, pricing models, and resource allocation.

**Who Belongs Here:**
- CFO and finance team
- Accounting and compliance
- Anyone responsible for resource stewardship

**What Finance Measures:**
- Traditional metrics: revenue, margin, cash flow
- Value metrics: value delivered per dollar invested, relationship health, capability building
- Which relationships create multiplying returns
- Where value compounds versus dissipates

**Leadership:** Chief Financial Officer—with expanded scope beyond revenue capture to include value delivery measurement.

## Why Three Orgs Instead of Fragmented Silos?

### What Traditional Structure Creates

**Marketing Department:** Generates "leads" and hands them off
**Sales Department:** Closes deals and hands them off
**Implementation Department:** Delivers and hands them off
**Account Management:** Retains and upsells
**Customer Success:** Prevents churn
**Support:** Handles problems
**Operations:** Each function has its own ops team

Each department optimizes for their metrics. Customers experience seven handoffs, context lost at each transition, relationships rebuilding with each new face.

### What Three-Org Structure Creates

**Customer Org:** Owns the entire relationship, no handoffs
**Operations Org:** Handles all coordination complexity
**Finance Org:** Stewards all resources

Customers experience one continuous relationship. Context accumulates rather than evaporates. Trust deepens rather than resets.

### The Math

Traditional: 7+ functional silos × coordination overhead × handoff friction = complexity explosion
Three-Org: 3 orgs × clear boundaries × AI-enabled coordination = sustainable simplicity

## The Value Steward Role

Within the Customer Org, the primary role is the **Value Steward**—humans partnered with AI who steward relationships through the entire Value Path.

**What Makes Value Stewards Different:**

| Traditional Roles | Value Steward |
|-------------------|---------------|
| SDRs recognize signals and set meetings | Full journey ownership from signal to championship |
| AEs navigate buying and close deals | No handoffs between stages |
| CSMs ensure retention and prevent churn | Deep relationship development over time |
| Each knows their stage, none owns the relationship | AI handles breadth; human provides depth |

**The Partnership Model:**

- **AI handles:** Research, documentation, coordination, pattern recognition, operational complexity
- **Human handles:** Judgment, trust-building, creative problem-solving, emotional intelligence

One Value Steward partnered with AI might steward 50-100 relationships through their entire journey—versus 500 "leads" through a single stage.

## Teaching the Three-Org Model to Clients

### When to Introduce

The Three-Org Model is most relevant for clients who:
- Experience pain from functional silos and handoff friction
- Are ready for organizational transformation, not just tool optimization
- Have leadership willing to challenge traditional structures
- Recognize that AI changes the economics of organization

### How to Position

**Frame as:** Organizational architecture for Value-Led Growth
**Not as:** Org chart redesign or restructuring exercise

**Emphasize:** The model eliminates handoffs and coordination overhead
**Not:** The model requires firing your marketing team

**Connect to:** Their experience of context loss and relationship friction
**Not:** Abstract organizational theory

### The Transformation Timeline

**Foundation:** When core relationships demonstrate the new model works
**Capability:** When teams operate confidently in the new structure
**Multiplication:** When results compound and transformation becomes self-sustaining

This is trust-based, not calendar-based. Organizations transform at the pace of genuine readiness, not consultant-driven project timelines.

## Usage Rules

### Naming Conventions

**Always use:**
- "Three-Org Model" (hyphenated)
- "Customer Org," "Operations Org," "Finance Org" (capitalized, no "the")
- "Value Steward" (not "account manager" or "customer success manager")

**Never use:**
- "Three Organization Model" (use "Org")
- "Sales Org" or "Marketing Org" (those don't exist in this model)
- Department language when describing the model

### Positioning Language

**DO say:**
- "The Customer Org owns the entire Value Path"
- "Operations enables the Customer Org to be effective"
- "Finance measures value delivered, not just revenue captured"

**DON'T say:**
- "The Customer Org is like a super sales team"
- "Operations is back-office support"
- "Finance handles the money stuff"

## Common Errors to Avoid

### Conceptual Errors

- ❌ Treating the Three-Org Model as just a new org chart
- ❌ Mapping traditional departments into the three orgs (Marketing → Customer Org, etc.)
- ❌ Assuming the model requires firing people (it requires reorganizing responsibility)
- ❌ Implementing the structure without the philosophy

### Language Errors

- ❌ Using "Sales" and "Marketing" to describe Customer Org functions
- ❌ Calling Operations Org "support" or "back-office"
- ❌ Describing Finance Org only in terms of money management

### Implementation Errors

- ❌ Creating handoffs within the Customer Org (defeats the purpose)
- ❌ Keeping Operations fragmented (Rev Ops, Marketing Ops, Sales Ops)
- ❌ Measuring only revenue without value accounting
